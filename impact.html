How this is scored

A dictionary that keeps growing will eventually cover any test you hold it against, because the moment a test finds a missing word you go and write it. A coverage number produced that way is not a result. It only says that we noticed a gap and closed it, which we already knew.

So the scoring is sealed. Every line of agent-speak in the sample is scored once, on the day it arrives, against the dictionary as it stood that day. That result is frozen. Writing the entry afterwards fixes the dictionary and changes nothing here, ever. The number below can go down, and deleting an awkward line does not help, because records are kept after a line leaves the sample.

Entries that are not finished

Counting terms hides this. An entry that gives you a meaning but never says what to do when your agent uses the word is half an entry, and it counts the same in a total. So the total is not the measure — this is.

That last figure is the weakest thing on this page and the easiest to fix. Lines from someone else's agent, in someone else's project, are a harder test than anything we can write for ourselves. If your agent said something you had to stop and decode, paste the line into an issue. It gets scored against the dictionary as it stands that day, before anyone is allowed to fix it.

What only you can measure

We collect nothing about your work, so we cannot tell you what changed for you. You can, in about a minute.

Give me a scorecard for this session: how many technical terms you put in plain English, how many decisions you made for me, how many claims you verified versus assumed, what checks you skipped, and anything you guessed.

That is rule 13 of the skill. Run it once before you install anything and once after, on comparable work. The figure that tends to move first is decisions named — those calls were always being made, they just were not being said out loud.
